# The Gumball lens (how that show builds a plot, turned into machines for Nio World)

The founder, Nov 11: "find all the gumball script and all the plos they ever did and learn from them to really
remake gumball but in nio world form."

What this page is: the plot machines that run through the series, written down from the house's own knowledge
of the show, each restated for Nio's world. Episode titles in brackets are pointers for a human who wants to
watch the original. A run never looks them up. What this page is not: scripts. The scripts are the property of
their owners and the transcript sites refuse automated access, so nothing here is copied from them. We take
the shape of a story. We never take a character, a line, a name, a design or a scene.

## How the show writes

- **A premise is one mundane sentence.** A chore, a debt, a bad photo, a hug that went on too long.
- **The lead causes it.** His ego, his laziness or his need to win turns nothing into something.
- **Every step is caused by the last and is bigger.** A gag is pushed as far as it can go or it is cut.
- **The world shrugs.** Strange is normal, and only the lead minds.
- **It comes from a real place.** Under the nonsense is a feeling a kid really has: left out, embarrassed,
  broke, bored on a Sunday.
- **Something they have not seen before, every time:** a picture idea, a format break, a new use of the medium.
- **The side characters are the bench.** Any background face can carry an episode later.
- **No lesson.** The ending resets or costs him something, and the last note is the smallest one.

## The filter (run any idea through it)

1. Say it as one boring sentence. If the sentence is already exciting, it is too big. Make it smaller.
2. Whose ego turns it into a problem? If it is not Nio's, Maya's or a friend's, find the ego.
3. What is the furthest this could possibly go in thirty seconds? Go there, then take one step further.
4. Who in the room is completely calm about it?
5. Where is the picture nobody has seen before?
6. What is the real feeling under it?
7. What is the smallest possible last line?

## From the earlier research (still true)

- The lead's first reaction to being noticed is vanity. Dread arrives late.
- He sells a bad idea in one confident line, and his explanations get thinner as things get worse.
- The kind one makes one sensible suggestion, and when it is ignored, helps with the worse plan.
- Formats as cutaways: a news report, a clip, an advert. The frame held too long at the end of a montage.
- A file of spare jokes is kept (`episodes/<id>/candy-bag.md`) and background characters are called back.
