# The style handoff (how the show we admire is written, turned into instructions for Nio World)

The founder, Nov 11: do for the writing what was done for the look. Go to the source, have the style described
precisely, and write from that. This page is that description. It sits beside `bible/gumball-lens.md` (the plot
machines) and replaces nothing there.

**We take the craft. We take no character, name, place, line, design, plot or title format.** If a draft
reminds you of a scene that exists, throw it away.

## What this is built on (so nobody trusts it more than it deserves)

- **Read at the source, Nov 11:** two 2025 interviews with the creator and the series director (Tell-Tale TV);
  a 2014 interview with the creator (Skwigly); a 2020 storyboarding panel with the show's series director and
  one of its board artists (Cartoon Brew); the production and reception notes on Wikipedia; and the plot
  summaries of all 80 episodes of seasons 2 and 3, which were sorted by type.
- **Not read:** the scripts and transcripts. The sites that host them, and the big trope and award sites,
  refuse automated access, and the standing rule is not to work around that. Nothing here is copied from a
  script.
- **Everything about line-level dialogue, pacing and sound below is the writer's own close knowledge of the
  show**, checked against those sources where they speak. Numbers marked *(estimate)* are estimates. If the
  founder can paste three or four scenes he loves, this page gets sharper.

## 1. Where a story comes from

- **The makers' own embarrassment.** The creator calls it the recipe: the room digs up each person's most
  shameful or awkward memory from growing up and turns it into comedy. The characters began as caricatures of
  his own family and friends. *This is exactly what the founder's stories are.* Rule for us: **a premise
  starts from something that happened to a real person and still makes them wince or laugh.**
- **One ordinary noun.** A remote, a password, a fridge chart, a phone, a hug, a lie. If the premise needs a
  sentence with "and" in it, it is two premises.
- **Steal from life, constantly.** The creator: everything he sees goes into the show. Keep a list.
- **A spare-joke file.** The room keeps a document of good jokes with no home yet, sometimes for years, until
  the right scene turns up. Ours is `episodes/<id>/candy-bag.md`, and one shared one for the series.

## 2. What kinds of story, and how often

Sorted from the 80 episodes read (each counted once, by its main engine):

| Kind | Share | In Nio World |
|---|---|---|
| A family member's problem or pride drives it | about 1 in 6 | A friend's problem or pride drives it (Sami's dignity, Farid's certainty) |
| A small thing balloons until the whole town is in it | about 1 in 7 | A bit or a favour balloons until the building, the campus or the map is in it |
| A side character gets the episode | about 1 in 9 | Any friend in `bible/world.md`, with Nio in the back of the shot |
| The two leads fall out over something petty | about 1 in 10 | Nio and Maya, Nio and Dev |
| A small habit or mishap escalates | about 1 in 10 | The zip. The contacts. One wave at the wrong person |
| School and authority | about 1 in 13 | The professor with advice, the landlord, the group project |
| A quest, an errand, a mystery | about 1 in 16 | Getting someone home. Finding the party. Whose shoe is this |
| A format or parody episode | about 1 in 16 | An episode told as an album, a map, a group chat, a game |
| A lesson or a rule that backfires | about 1 in 20 | Someone gives Nio advice and he follows it exactly |
| Internet and technology | about 1 in 25 | Used as behaviour, see section 7 |
| Holiday | about 1 in 25 | `engine/calendar.md` |
| Romance | about 1 in 40 | Rare. And Nio does not understand it |

Two things to copy from that table: **romance is rare**, and **the leads are the cause** far more often than
an outside villain is (about 1 in 25).

## 3. How a story is built

For eleven minutes they do this; for our 35 to 60 seconds we do the same shape with one of everything.

1. **Start inside an ordinary moment**, already going. No scene-setting. The first line is someone mid-thought.
2. **The itch**, within the first tenth of the running time: a small annoyance, want or embarrassment.
3. **The lead's theory.** He decides what this *means* and what he will do, and says so with total
   confidence to the sincere one. The theory is wrong in an interesting way. This is the engine: **his ego
   turns the itch into a project.**
4. **Three escalations, each caused by the fix for the last.** Never "and then". Always "so". Each one is
   bigger in scale: me, then us, then everybody.
5. **The tilt.** About two-thirds through, the story changes size or kind: the town is involved, or it has
   become a different genre, and the characters carry on as though that is reasonable.
6. **The set piece.** The furthest the idea can go. The series director's rule in the room: take the joke to
   its extreme on purpose.
7. **The real second.** One true feeling, connected to why he started. Short.
8. **Out fast.** A reset, an ironic price, or a hard cut on the last joke. Almost never a lesson; when a
   lesson is spoken, it is the wrong one. The ending is usually within a few seconds of the peak.

Tests: could you remove any step without the next one breaking? Then it was an "and then". Is anyone other
than a lead causing the trouble? Then find the ego.

## 4. Who the people are, and what makes a pair work

- **The lead** is the cause. Quick, sarcastic, vain, a schemer with a theory about everything, lazy about the
  simple fix and tireless about the complicated one. Small personal things devastate him and real danger
  bores him. Under it, one second of honest insecurity an episode. He loves his people and shows it badly.
- **The sincere one** completes him: kind, literal, hopeful, loyal enough to help with the bad plan after
  suggesting the good one. He is not a straight man; he is funny because he means it. A few times a season
  he snaps or is brutally honest, and it is an event.
- **They are a double act.** They agree instantly on terrible ideas, finish each other's bits, argue about
  nothing at speed, and one sincere line from the kind one punctures whatever the lead just built.
- **Everybody else is one clear idea plus one worry.** A single way of looking, a single way of talking, one
  thing they want too much. Each runs on their own logic and nobody remarks on it.
- **Anyone in the background can carry an episode.** The makers say their favourite episodes are the ones that
  take a secondary character and show who they are.
- **Adults are large children with jobs.** Competence and rage, or joy and no sense.

How this lands on our people is in `bible/characters.md` and `bible/world.md`. The founder's Nio differs from
that lead in ways that matter: Nio is capable, laid back, humble when truly beaten, and generous with his
hands. Keep those. Borrow the engine: **ego, theory, escalation.**

## 5. How they talk

- **Fast, and to each other.** Lines are reactions to the last line. Nobody waits their turn.
- **The lead's long line is a run.** It starts reasonable and gets more specific and more unhinged as it goes,
  usually a comparison nobody asked for. It ends on the smallest word.
- **Sarcasm by exact restatement.** He repeats what you said, accurately, so you hear it.
- **Over-specific detail.** Never "a lot". The number, the brand of the feeling, the day of the week.
- **Understatement after disaster.** The building is gone. "Okay, so that's one way."
- **The sincere line as a punchline.** The kind one says something true and plain straight after the rant.
- **Interruptions and overlaps**, and two people saying the same thing at once then looking at each other.
- **The anti-joke.** A set-up that is answered literally.
- **A list that goes wrong at the third item** (as events or pictures; a spoken list of three is a tell).
- **Saying the convention out loud, rarely.** "We're not doing a montage." Then the montage.
- **A held look.** After the fastest exchange, two seconds of nothing on a face.
- **People sound their age and their time.** The kids talk like kids who live online, lightly: address words
  ("dude" there; "bro" here), dropped words, the odd current phrase used the way a person uses it. **Slang is
  seasoning, never the joke, and never stacked.** An adult using it wrong is a joke.
- **Nobody is witty in the same way.** Cover the names and you still know who spoke.

## 6. Pacing

- **Dense.** Something funny or new roughly every five to ten seconds *(estimate)*, and no scene that only
  delivers information. For our vertical short: every three to four seconds, and no shot without a face
  changing or something moving.
- **Cut on the joke.** The shot ends the frame the joke lands. Reactions are a quick take, then gone.
- **Smash cuts carry the plot.** "There is no way I'm doing that." Cut: he is doing that.
- **Montage compresses the middle** of an escalation, and the last image of a montage is held a beat too long.
- **Slowness is a weapon because it is rare.** One or two held silences an episode, on a face, after speed.
- **The picture is boarded before the words are final.** The show is storyboard-driven: the board artists set
  the comic timing, rewrite dialogue and add picture gags. Their rule for boards: do not just draw what the
  script says. So a script here is a proposal to the picture, and a joke with no words wins.
- **Plain staging for jokes.** The series director's note: a showy angle can overpower a joke. Flat, clear,
  face readable. Save the dramatic shot for the moment the lead is taking himself too seriously.
- **Expression is extreme and brief.** A face goes somewhere impossible for a few frames, or the drawing style
  changes for one beat, then snaps back.

## 7. Trends, the internet and the real world

- **Satirise the behaviour, not the meme.** The show's internet episodes are about what people do: a video of
  you gets out and removing it spreads it; being rude in comments has a face on the other end; a lonely adult
  finds an app and uses it with frightening sincerity. A named meme is dead by air date. A behaviour lasts.
- **Invent the brands.** Their world has its own video site, its own social site, its own shops. Ours has PXI,
  which is real, and everything else is made up.
- **A trend arrives through a person who lives it sincerely**, in front of a lead who cannot believe it. Never
  through a character doing the trend at us.
- **Parody a format, not a title.** A fight staged like an anime, a chore shot like a heist film, a
  conversation as a dating-show edit. One beat, full commitment, then back.
- **Heavier subjects are allowed if they are played for fun and through a character**: being broke, being left
  out, growing up. The makers say this on record about an episode on inequality.
- **Current things for Nio World, as behaviours:** the vocabulary of dating (a break, talking, soft launch),
  group chat politics (who was left on read, who was removed), location sharing, everyone filming and nobody
  sending, the friend who has an AI for everything, the gym as a personality, the "main character" walk.

## 8. Sound and music

- **The score plays the lead's delusion completely straight.** A trip to the corner shop gets the full epic.
  Then it **stops dead** on the joke, and the silence is the punchline. (The show's composer was nominated
  for an Annie for this work.)
- **Music changes genre with the scene**, instantly, to match what the character thinks is happening.
- **Real-world sounds on cartoon events.** The more impossible the picture, the more ordinary the sound.
- **Voices sound like real young people**: breaths, cracks, laughs they did not mean, overlaps.
- **Room tone during the awkward hold.** A fridge hum. A distant car. Never music.
- **Songs are set pieces there.** We do not do them: no sung lyrics.
- **For Nio World:** the music is what is in his earbuds, so it can be as big as he feels and it cuts when an
  earbud comes out. Every voice line gets an acting note with a breath, a crack or a laugh in it where it
  fits. One silence an episode is planned in the script.

## 9. What a draft must show (the checks this page adds)

1. The premise is one ordinary noun, from a real embarrassment.
2. Nio, Maya or a friend causes it. Name the ego.
3. A confident wrong theory is spoken in the first quarter.
4. Three "so" steps, each bigger. No "and then".
5. A tilt in size or kind.
6. One run from the lead, one sincere puncture from the kind one.
7. One smash cut. One held face. One joke with no words.
8. The music stops dead once.
9. A trend, if there is one, walks in as a person.
10. Out within three seconds of the peak. No lesson.
