# Story doctrine (read before any premise or script; this is what makes an episode worth watching)

Founder, Nov 10, on the first pilot build ("Not Me"): "i had no way of tracking what happened or understanding
the plot. it did not look like anything interesting to be honest ... i dont even know what the point of this new
plot you made is i need you to have the engine produce genuinly interesting stories." And on what the series is:
"nio making a platform he thinks would have solved his problems and will in future and inviting us to use it."

A joke method is not a story. `bible/humor.md` says how a scene is funny. This page says why anyone watches.

## 1. The series in one breath

**Nio moved to a city where he knew nobody, kept finding out about everything after it happened, and was not in
a single photo. So he built PXI, the thing he is sure would have fixed all of it. The series is him using it to
build a life, one night at a time, and asking us to come.**

- Nio is PXI's maker. He is proud of it the way a man is proud of a plan. He talks to us about it.
- Every episode is one of his problems, his fix for it, and what actually happens.
- PXI works. What goes sideways is Nio. He usually gets what he needed and not what he wanted.
- Over the series his world visibly fills up: people, places, photos, things he has been to. That is the journey
  people follow for.

## 2. The shape of every episode

| When | What the viewer gets |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3 s | **The hook:** one picture that asks a question, with motion and sound |
| By 5 s | **The problem and the want**, said plainly. Usually Nio to the camera, in one line |
| By 10 s | **The plan:** what he is going to do about it, and the PXI feature it runs on |
| Middle | **Three steps**, each caused by the last and worse than the last, with the goal always in sight |
| Near the end | **The turn:** something the viewer did not see coming and then feels was always coming |
| End | **The payoff:** he gets what he needed, at a cost. One image that stays |
| After | **The tag:** Nio to us, the ask |

The problem and the plan are said out loud at the top, the way Nathan Fielder states the business problem and
the plan before anything happens. That is what makes the rest legible. Comedy that hides its premise is just
confusing.

## 3. The tests a premise has to pass

Write the answer to each in one line. If a line is hard to write, the premise is not ready.

1. **Recognition.** Is this a problem the viewer has had this month? (Found out about the party from other
   people's photos. The plan died in the group chat. Standing alone at a thing. The only photo of you is bad.
   Nobody to go with. A holiday away from home.)
2. **A want you can film.** A goal with a finish line the camera can see: ten people come, one good photo
   exists, he is invited back. Not a feeling and not "avoid embarrassment".
3. **Stakes and a clock.** What happens if he fails, to whom, by when. Tonight is better than this week.
4. **PXI is his answer.** The feature is the plan itself, the thing he reaches for because he built it. If the
   app only delivers a notification that starts the plot, it fails.
5. **The engine.** Does the plan keep producing trouble without help? Three steps should come easily.
6. **The turn.** What does the viewer not expect, that is still fair?
7. **The point.** One sentence: what the viewer feels at the end, and what is different in Nio's world.
8. **The retell.** Could a viewer tell a friend the episode in one sentence and have it sound worth watching?
   "A guy with no friends throws himself a birthday and invites the whole map" passes. "A guy is in a photo
   and something happens with a family" does not.
9. **The send.** Who sends this to whom, and what do they type with it?

## 4. The tests a script has to pass

- **The cold read.** A reader who knows nothing reads only the short-cut script and answers: who is this, what
  does he want, why does it matter, what is the plan, what happened, what was the point, would you send it. The
  answers must match the header the writer wrote. If the reader cannot say the point, the script fails, however
  funny the lines are.
- **Sound off.** The same questions from the pictures and the on-screen text alone. Most people meet the show
  with the sound off, and the founder met the first pilot that way. Every release is subtitled and reads silent.
- **No homework.** The episode needs nothing from an earlier one. The series rewards people who follow, and
  never punishes people who do not.
- **Ends somewhere new.** Something in Nio's world has changed and is written into `bible/timeline.md`: a person
  he now knows, a place he now goes, an album that now exists.

## 5. What "Not Me" got wrong (keep this as the warning)

- Nothing told the viewer who Nio was or what he wanted, so there was nobody to root for.
- The stake was private embarrassment in front of strangers we would never see again.
- PXI only sent the notification. It was not his answer to anything.
- It ended with nothing changed that a viewer would care about.
- It was shown without sound, and it did not read without sound.

Small stakes are fine. Unstated stakes are not. A deadpan show still tells you what the man wants.

## 6. The journey

- The series moves in **chapters**, each with a goal the viewer can hold: first, not being a stranger here;
  then, people coming to his thing; then, his thing being the place to be. The founder names the chapters.
- Things that carry across episodes and can be seen on screen: who is in his circle, the albums he is in, the
  stamps in his passport, the places he is now a regular. They go up slowly and they never reset.
- People return. Someone met in one episode is in the background of the next, then has a line, then is a friend.
- An episode asks viewers to follow only when there is a real next thing to follow for, and names it.

## 7. The two pinned reels

Owned by the pilot thread. What each has to do:

- **The pilot:** one complete episode that passes every test above. A stranger finishes it knowing who Nio is,
  what he made, and wanting the next one.
- **"What this is":** Nio to the camera, 30 seconds or less. Where he came from, the problems he had, the thing
  he built, what we are going to watch him do with it, and the ask. Flat, proud, specific, a little too
  detailed. It is the one place the show explains itself.

## 8. Canon reset

deletes it, are from before this and are not canon unless the founder brings them back. Their plants in
`bible/callbacks.md` are parked.
