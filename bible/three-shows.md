# Three shows, three jobs (plot, humor, eerie) and how Nio World talks

The founder, Nov 13: "inspire your plots from regular show and humor and characters from gumball, eeriness
from adventure time." This page says what each of those does that we take, and how to make it ours. Read it
with `bible/gumball-style.md`. We take the method only: no character, line, name, design or plot.

## Plot, from Regular Show

The engine every episode of that show runs on, confirmed by its press and reviews: **a boring chore, a
mundane frustration from work or school, and the moment someone tries to dodge or cheat it, a supernatural or
impossible thing turns up and the stakes go to the end of the world.** Then it ends fast, and the chore is
still there.

- **Start with something everyone has had to do.** Office hours. Laundry. Finding the party. Getting in.
- **The dodge opens the door.** The impossible thing arrives because the lead took the shortcut.
- **The impossible thing has rules, and everyone in it obeys them calmly.** A waiting room with no clock
  hands has a ticket machine that works.
- **It gets an epic.** Score, scale, a fight or a chase, played straight.
- **It is beaten with a small human skill** the lead had all along: here, Nio's hands (cube speed, cooking,
  the piano, a camera, football).
- **Out in seconds.** One last turn of the screw and cut. The chore is still waiting, or it was never the
  point.

## Humor and characters, from Gumball

Already in `bible/gumball-style.md`. The short version for the room: faces that go somewhere impossible for a
few frames, a world full of people with their own strange logic that nobody questions, the lead's confident
wrong theory, and people talking fast *to each other*, never trading set-ups and comebacks.

## Eerie, from Adventure Time

Its creator's own words for it: candy on the surface and dark underneath. How it gets there:

- **The detail nobody mentions.** A water bottle and a planner from 2019 on an empty seat, under dust. The
  episode never says what happened to its owner. The viewer does the rest.
- **Withhold the explanation forever.** Never say how the place works or why it is there.
- **Sound drops out.** Music cuts to room tone: a fluorescent hum, a fridge, breathing.
- **The cute thing is sad.** The monster is lonely. The person stuck there is calm about it.
- **One question that lands too hard**, asked kindly, and then the episode moves on.
- **It is played sincerely by the animation.** Faces hold. Nobody winks.

Every episode does not need it. One in three has an eerie stretch of a few seconds, and it is always inside
a funny episode, never instead of one.

## How they talk (Nio World is now)

What the founder asked for: like Gumball's characters, and like Gen Z. The rules:

- **No set-up and comeback.** A line that exists so the next line can top it is cut. ("Eighty percent you
  regret this." "I've never regretted anything." is the example the founder called corny.) People react to
  what just happened, in their own logic, and the joke is the situation or the face.
- **Specific, odd, true.** "There's a fry." beats any clever line.
- **Slang is how they talk, not what they talk about.** One or two terms in a scene, from the right person,
  dropped in passing. An adult using it slightly wrong is a joke. A young character explaining it is a defect.
- **Founder, Nov 13: keep slang out of the early episodes.** The comedy comes from the situation and the
  faces, like Gumball and Regular Show. When a term is used later, it is used with confidence (calling someone
  a high tier normie, which is a decent rank; the insult is being a low tier chud), never as a character's
  insecurity, and never as the punchline.
- **Current terms the room can use (Nov 2026), in passing:** cooked, crash out, aura (and losing it), mogged,
  mogging, looksmaxxing, "it's giving", delulu, the ick, chopped, glazing, twin, unc, NPC, side quest, main
  character, "be so fr", "no shot", lowkey, highkey, "we're so back", "it's over", touch grass, ratio, valid,
  bet, say less, "chat, is this real". From the looks-rating corner of the internet, as mainstream jokes only:
  HTN, MTN, LTN, "mogged by the floor". **Never** the ideology those came from, never anything sexual (no
  "gyatt", no "huzz"), never a slur.
- **Comments sections are their own character.** When the show shows comments, they are short, merciless and
  very specific, the way a real comments section is.
- **Sources, to refresh the list every month:** the open Gen Z slang dataset on Hugging Face
  (`MLBtrio/genz-slang-dataset`), the slang tracker at `github.com/slangAI/slang`, and the year's slang
  round-ups. A term older than a year gets checked before it is used. The founder's ear wins over all of them.
