# Gumball / Darwin reference (research, Nov 9)

Sources: Wikipedia (show, character list, season episode synopses), Wikiquote seasons 1–6, AV Club, Screen Rant,
Bubbleblabber reviews, Skwigly + TellTale TV interviews with Ben Bocquelet / Erik Fountain. Several fan sites refuse
automated access; nothing here comes from getting around that. Personality and devices only — no lines, names, designs.

## Gumball (→ Nio)
- **Ego first, fear second.** His first reaction to being noticed is vanity; dread arrives late.
- **Total confidence in bad plans**, sold in one line. Lies by over-reassuring ("absolutely fine, no trouble here").
- **Rationalises everything** with confident pseudo-logic; the explanations get thinner as things get worse.
- **Lazy, phase-y, idealistic**; his phases never last.
- **Panic grammar**: a stammered word repeated, a fake-calm sentence, then a scream. Short enough to be cut off.
- **Inner life**: mostly muttered asides and talking to the universe, BUT he also thinks while someone else is in the
  room (the joke is the gap between what he thinks and what he can say), and he glances to the corner/camera. Use
  sparingly: one look to the lens per episode, a beat of deadpan, no line.
- Sarcasm grows over the seasons; underneath he's loyal and protective.

## Darwin (→ Maya)
- Optimistic, kind to a fault, literal: answers the words, not the meaning; asks plain clarifying questions.
- Notices the concrete detail the lead missed; that detail is often the scariest/funniest thing.
- Gives one sensible suggestion; when rejected, immediately helps with the worse plan.
- Scared = loud disbelief or a whisper; occasional dry zinger.

## The show's devices
- Mundane premise → over-engineered plan → escalation → dark or absurd button. No lesson, no tidy resolution.
- The world treats absurd things as normal; only the lead notices.
- Background gags the lead misses; montages that end on a frame held too long; formats as cutaways (news, viral
  clips, ads); the medium breaking (styles change, the episode glitches) when reality cracks.
- "Take a joke to the most furthest extreme" (Fountain). Keep a spare-joke file; call back recurring characters.
- Awkward silence as a set piece: hold 8–12 frames of dead air after the worst line.

## Do not
- Narrate what we can see. Explain the joke. Make the lead "stress" in tidy self-aware sentences.
- Give every line a punchline. Use rule-of-three lists. Let anyone be calm and articulate in a crisis.
