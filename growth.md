# What to post and how we learn

Goal: 300 followers on @pxi.app in about 8 weeks, and a viewer hooked enough that the second video they see makes
them download the app.

## Three phases

| Phase | Weeks | Target | What happens | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Find the voice | 1 to 2 | 50 | City keyword in the profile name. 5 videos and 2 carousels a week. Try 5 hook types once each. Trial-reel every main reel | One post at 2x the average views |
| Repeat what works | 3 to 5 | 150 | Keep the top 2, drop the bottom 2. The Thursday weekend map. Ask 3 organizers for Collabs. Test one ending a week | 150 followers, else repeat a week |
| Borrow audiences | 6 to 8 | 300 | Collab posts every week. Winners again as photo posts. Double down on the best hook | |

## Who it is for

1. **Someone looking for something to do.** Most of what we post, on every network. The job: they open Discover.
2. **Someone hosting at home** (a birthday, a game night, a housewarming): the invite by link, and the album
   everyone shoots into.
3. **Organizers**, who run events for a living. They are on LinkedIn (connected in Metricool), and they see the
   guest posts too. Say only what pxispace.com's own host pages say. Never a fee comparison.

Of every ten pieces, at least seven are for the first person and are about Discover.

## Trends

Each week pick up to two things everyone is posting about and make our version. Borrow the format, never the
property: no characters, logos, fonts or music from a game, show or brand, and our own cast in every role. Nothing
about a real person's private life. A public moment (a launch, a keynote, a meme) is fine when the joke is on us.

## Hooks (first 2 seconds; never the same type twice in a row)

Every hook says who it is for in its first words. A piece about a city calls the city and the need out loud, the
way the founder put it: "are you in [city] looking for things to do? we got you." The types below are ways to do
that, never a way around it.

A question ("why does everyone leave at 1:40?"), a mid-story cold open that rewinds, a myth check ("nothing to do
in LA on a Tuesday"), did you know ("did you know there's a free rave in an arcade this Saturday?"), a fun fact
about the city or the night, a list that holds one back ("four things this weekend, one of them is free"), "here
are tonight's and there are more", POV, only-here (a real listing), a ranked list, an identity quiz, a mystery
object. No logo, no title card, no greeting.

The same piece can be cut twice with two different hooks; the first two seconds are the cheapest thing to test.

## Endings (exactly one ask a piece, and the week uses all of them)

- **Comment:** a question one word can answer ("what's your group chat doing tonight?", "which one are you going to?").
- **Send:** "send this to the friend who always says idk".
- **Follow:** with the reason ("LA's weekend drops here every Thursday").
- **Download:** "it's free, it's called PXI", over the store badges.
- **Profile:** "tonight's list is on our page".
- **Save:** for a list someone will want on Friday.

The ask is this piece's own last line, said the way a friend would and tied to its pick or its joke. If the
sentence could end any video ("send this to the friend who..."), reviewers read it as bait: rewrite it.
`post.md` names the hook type and the ask of each piece, so Sunday's run can compare them.

## Being found (the founder, Nov 24: "show up when people type the best key words like things to do in la")

People search TikTok, Instagram and YouTube the way they search Google, and each one reads a video's spoken words,
the words on screen, its caption and its title. So every piece is built around **one search phrase**, the exact
words someone types, and that phrase goes in five places:

1. **Said** in the first line, as close to word for word as speech allows ("Looking for things to do in LA this
   weekend? We got you.").
2. **On screen** in the first two seconds (the caption of that line).
3. **First** in every network's caption, word for word, before anything clever.
4. **The YouTube title** (and its description's first sentence, and its tags).
5. **The alt text**, and one hashtag made of it (#thingstodoinla).

`post.md` names it ("Search phrase: things to do in la this weekend") and `python3 studio/check.py find
videos/<slug>` fails a piece that does not carry it. After the phrase, the caption says the picks in plain words
(day, name, neighbourhood, price) and what PXI is for in the words people search ("free app to find things to do
near you"). Instagram up to 5 hashtags, TikTok 3 to 5. No links or codes in TikTok captions; the link is in the bio.

**What people type** (Google's keyword planner for the United States, monthly searches, read Nov 24 through the
ads account; the same words rule the apps' own search boxes):

| Phrase | A month | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| things to do in la (also: stuff to do in la) | 74,000 | any LA piece that is not tied to a day |
| things to do in los angeles | 33,100 | the YouTube description and tags |
| things to do in la this weekend | 22,200 | the weekend reel |
| places to go in la | 18,100 | a piece about one place or neighbourhood |
| fun things to do in la | 14,800 | a "One night" piece that is odd or funny |
| things to do in la today (search boxes also offer: tonight) | 12,100 | stories and same-day pieces |
| free things to do in la | 6,600, little competition | any pick that is free |
| things to do in downtown la | 5,400 | a piece set downtown |

Search boxes finish "things to do in la this weekend" with: for adults, for couples, for free, at night, with
family. Each is a piece: the same weekend, picked for that person. New York follows the same shapes ("things to
do in nyc this weekend"; YouTube also offers "what to do in nyc this weekend").

**Learning what ranks.** Sunday's run reads YouTube's traffic sources in Metricool (how much came from search),
refreshes this table with the keyword planner for the cities we post about, and logs each piece's phrase beside
its hook and ask. TikTok's and Instagram's own search numbers are not in Metricool: the founder can open TikTok's
Creator Search Insights on the phone (it shows what people search and where few videos answer) and paste what it
shows into the thread. That is a human job to ask for, never to guess at.

**New people every post.** A piece that changes one thing (a second hook, a different ask, the same weekend "for
couples") can go out on Instagram as a trial reel: it is shown to people who do not follow us and stays off the
grid, so a test never costs the profile. The winner is posted again the normal way.

## Paid (the founder, Nov 24: "go soft for now ... push ads for highest roi not just for the sake of it")

Organic first. No ad runs until a post earns it. At most $150 a month, for everything paid on every platform put
together, and only where it returns the most.

- **The sign.** A post that does exceptionally well on its own: at least three times the account's usual reach
  two days after it went out, with sends or saves above the usual too. Sunday's run looks for it; so does any
  thread that reads the numbers. No sign, no spend.
- **Then.** That one post is put in front of more people like the ones who watched it: Instagram, the posted reel
  itself (so its likes and comments stay on it), one city, a few dollars a day, for a week. People who watch most
  of it become the audience for the download ask. TikTok's ads start at $20 a day for one ad group, so TikTok stays
  organic under this cap.
- **Always.** A campaign is created paused, with what it will cost and what it is meant to show; the founder
  switches it on. Spend for the month and what it bought go in Sunday's report. Never a fee comparison.

## Audio

The file carries the voice and small sound effects. Instagram reels get a licensed library track at scheduling
(Metricool `audioConfiguration`, search by word, keep the voice on top: track 15 to 25). TikTok photo posts:
`autoAddMusic`. TikTok videos keep their own sound; a trending sound is added by hand in the app. Never AI music.

## Reading the numbers (Sunday's run, `runs.md`)

Sends per reach, trial-reel skip rate, completion, saves, comments, follows per post and profile visits. Keep the
hook and format of the top 2, drop the bottom 2, change one thing a week (hook, ending, format, length, time). One
line per test in `STATE.md`. Judge the strategy after 30 posts; never delete a low performer.

Human jobs to flag, never fake: replying to comments in the first hour, commenting on local venues and organizers,
asking organizers for Collabs and photo permission.
