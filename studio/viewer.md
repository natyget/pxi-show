# The viewer

Every piece is made for one person and checked by that person: 18 to 30, scrolling with a thumb, sound maybe off,
who has never heard of PXI, Nio or anyone in our cast, and owes us nothing. The maker writes and designs as that
viewer. The founder reviews as that viewer too, so anything the founder would catch, the maker should catch first.

## Before the voice is made: read the script as the viewer

For every line ask, and rewrite until each answer is fine:

- Do I know in the first two seconds that this is for me? If it is about a city, the first words say the city
  and what I am after ("Are you in LA, looking for things to do this weekend? We got you."). A clever line that
  could open any video is not a hook.
- Is it said to me? ("You open PXI", not a name I don't know. Names belong in Nio World stories, where I meet the people.)
- Is it said at the speed of what it is? Information comes quick and bright; only a story takes its time.
- Would a friend say it this way, out loud?
- Does the speaker sound sure? A narrator never sounds puzzled by his own app.
- Do I already see this on screen? Then the line goes. The voice gives the reason to go; the card gives the facts.
  One fact may be said the way a friend says it ("about twenty-five bucks").
- Do I learn what the app does without being told it is an app?

## Before anything is shown: look at every frame as the viewer

- Would a real person have made this? A flyer looks like a host made it: one idea, the day big, the time small.
  A chat looks like my chat. A screen looks like the app on my phone.
- Is there anything I would not put there if I made it by hand: a bar, a tag, a label, a second line saying the first?
- Do I know who is talking and why each thing happens?
- Has the picture changed in the last four seconds? The app held on screen under a voice reads as a screen
  recording. The app is the proof inside a story, a few seconds at a time, and it fills most of the frame when it is on.
- Is there one moment I would send to a friend?

## The stranger (a fresh reviewer, for every finished video)

Give a reviewer who has seen nothing the contact sheets (`studio/sheet.py`: the first three seconds four times a
second, then a frame at every cut and at least every 1.25 s) and the spoken lines, and nothing else. Ask: what happened and what did the person want; what is the app and what would you search for; where would
you scroll away; what confused you; which line or picture reads as an ad or as machine-made; what looks wrong;
would you send it to a friend. A FAIL is fixed and reviewed once more. Two FAILs and the piece is dropped. A new
cut the founder asked for starts the count again. The stranger sees stills, so say in one line what moves in any
shot that holds for more than two seconds.
